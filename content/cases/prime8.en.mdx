# A distributor born small that had to enter the market looking big.

### The brand, the design system and the B2B purchase were designed together, because for those who resell, trust is part of the product.

**My role**\
UX/UI Design and Branding. Research, visual identity, design system and e-commerce.

**Period**\
2023

**Clients**\
Resellers and small and medium technology companies

Prime8 was born in 2023 as a new business unit of Zoom Holding: the group's distribution channel. It arrived with a heavyweight portfolio: Huawei, realme smartphones and Intelbras telephony. It needed its own online sales channel and a brand that did not yet exist. Its market entry had to communicate a major player from day one.

#### If you read only one part

## Prime8 had products from leading brands, but no brand of its own, no channel and no way to sell to businesses without friction.

## What I did was treat brand, system and purchase journey as one thing, starting from who buys and why.

## The result was an identity with usage rules, a documented design system and a B2B e-commerce with credit, SLA and CNAE verification built into the journey.

---

#### The problem

Selling to a business is not selling to a person.

A reseller buys to make a profit, compares margins and needs to trust the supplier before opening an account. An IT manager buys for their own company and wants it sorted quickly. Both land on the same site.

- A new brand, with no track record, selling alongside names like Huawei and Intelbras.
- No online sales channel of its own.
- Two audiences with different motivations: resale and purchase for own use.
- Credit, payment terms and a CNAE check (the Brazilian company activity code) on every company registration, steps that usually turn into a form and an abandoned cart.
- Customer service and after-sales that depended on correct data from sign-up onwards.

The risk was designing a generic e-commerce with a new logo. What Prime8 needed first was to know who it was talking to.

#### What I did in research

### I split the audience in two before designing a single screen.

With desk research and interviews with users and with people experienced in the business, I mapped the audience by what, who, why, when and where. Two proto-personas came out: the owner of a computer shop who buys to resell, and the IT manager who buys for her own company. Both were small and medium businesses, but the reason for buying changed everything: margin for one, speed and support for the other.

![Research board with personas and audience map](/assets/img/prime8/pesquisas.png)

### I used the research to cut fields, not just to illustrate.

The mapping already pointed to a concrete optimisation: validate sign-up with Google and ask only for the company name and registration number at the start. The heavy verification, credit and CNAE, happens later, once the person has already decided they want to buy.

---

#### What I did on the brand

### I wrote usage rules, not just a logo.

With the journey, the personas, the empathy map and the analysis of the main competitors documented, I defined the palette, typography, supporting elements and design principles. Orange is mandatory and takes up little space, green and the greys hold the layout together, and every combination has a defined priority and proportion. A brand without rules falls apart by the third piece.

![Brand guidelines with application examples](/assets/img/prime8/id-visual-2.png)

#### What I did in the product

### I turned the brand into a design system before prototyping.

With the identity defined, I created the product's design system and documented every step of handling and optimisation. Icons, payment methods, colours, shadows and grid came from the same source as the guidelines. So the prototype was born already looking like the brand.

![Prime8 design system foundations](/assets/img/prime8/foto1.jpg)

### I designed the whole flow, from sign-up to after-sales.

I mapped home, category, product, cart, three-step checkout, sign-up and account, plus the trust pages: returns, delivery options, payment and policies. For a new brand, those pages are part of the sale.

![Prototype map with the e-commerce flows](/assets/img/prime8/prototipo1.png)

### I made sign-up an advantage, not a barrier.

The price stays locked for anyone without an account, and the invitation to become a reseller appears on every page. Instead of hiding the B2B requirement, the journey presents it as access to special conditions. Credit, payment SLA and CNAE verification come later, once the person already has a reason to carry on.

![Components with the price locked until sign-up](/assets/img/prime8/foto2.png)

![Product page with the reseller banner](/assets/img/prime8/foto6.png)

### I iterated every week, with a test at the end of each cycle.

The prototypes went through weekly cycles, with user tests at the end of each one and adjustments based on feedback from internal stakeholders and users. The goal was an objective, frictionless journey that felt safe both for the buyer and for customer service and after-sales.

![Prime8 e-commerce on mobile](/assets/img/prime8/mobile.png)

#### What changed

### Prime8 went from zero to a brand, a system and a channel that speak the same language, and ran on them for more than two years.

The identity has rules anyone can apply. The design system links the guidelines to the component. And the purchase journey treats the B2B requirements as part of the experience, not as an obstacle at the end of it.

In that period, Prime8 received awards from Huawei as an international reseller partner and, still in 2023, Gold certification as a Huawei eKit distributor in Brazil. Later, a restructuring at Zoom Holding shut down some business units, Prime8 among them.

![Prime8 e-commerce on laptop and mobile](/assets/img/prime8/fotocapa.png)

---

#### In hindsight

### I would start by measuring earlier. The research guided every decision, but I never defined how we would know whether the sign-up journey was working after launch.

### Doing brand and product together taught me that guidelines alone are not enough. The brand only holds when it lives inside the components.
