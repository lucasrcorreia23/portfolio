# Companies with scattered data decide in the dark. I designed, from scratch, a platform that brings the data together and puts AI agents to work pointing out the opportunity.

### The client did not want another dashboard. They wanted to know what to do with what they already had.

**My role**\
Product Designer. Discovery, information architecture, interface, design system and the experience with AI agents.

**Period**\
2024 to 2025

**Team**\
PM, data engineering, data specialists and Qlik partners

Decision Suite, by Fivelabs, is a platform for companies that want to make data-driven decisions. It brings visualisation, insights and analysis together in one place, with specialist AI agents for each area. When I joined, there was no product yet. There was a problem observed across several companies and a question: what, exactly, did they need to see?

![Decision Suite main banner](/assets/img/decision-suite/Hero-DS.jpg)

#### If you read only one part

## Companies had too much data, in too many places, and nobody to turn it into decisions.

## What I did was start from what clients were saying, prioritise ruthlessly and design a product that takes the information to the person, instead of waiting for them to go looking for it.

## The result was a platform with dashboards, alerts, requests for new dashboards and an assistant with specialist agents, built on a design system made together with data engineering.

---

#### The problem

The problem was not a lack of data. It was a lack of trust and context.

In the interviews, prospective clients described the same scenario in different words:

- "I lack the correct, clear information to make the decisions I need."
- "I have the same kind of information in several different places and I don't know which one is true."
- "I have plenty of dashboards in the company, but not with the information I need."
- "Having a data science office in the company is very expensive."
- "I don't have a skilled team to help me build the dashboards I need."

Each statement called for a different solution. The temptation was to answer all of them with dashboards. What the product needed first was to decide what it would not do.

#### What I did in discovery

### I started with the doubts, not the screens.

Together with the PM, I built a CSD matrix with the certainties, assumptions and doubts we had, and used it to plan the exploratory interviews. First we spoke with stakeholders and data specialists. Then with prospective clients, to see the problem in practice. What they said validated a good part of our hypotheses about the market.

### I prioritised with MoSCoW to have a pipeline, not a wish list.

With the interviews in hand, we ran a brainstorm, grouped the most relevant ideas and applied MoSCoW to build the prioritisation pipeline. In a product with nothing built yet, deciding the order matters as much as deciding what.

![Discovery and prioritisation artefacts](/assets/img/decision-suite/design-process.jpg)

### I adjusted the process for each module.

Because it was a project from scratch, the process varied with the module being prioritised. In general, I started with a functional flow to set the scope. Then came low-fidelity wireframes, validated quickly with the people involved. Only then the high-fidelity prototype, refined in cycles through to documentation and following the handoff.

#### What I did in the product

### I turned "I don't have a team to build dashboards" into a feature.

If the client has no data team, the product needs to play that role. I designed the new project request as a step by step: which indicator, what type of chart, where the data comes from and what the calculation rule is. The request arrives ready for engineering, and the client follows stages, cost and deadline inside the platform.

![New project request](/assets/img/decision-suite/take-07.jpg)

### I made the information go to the person.

A dashboard only helps whoever opens it. Alerts let people know when a goal is reached, when an insight appears or when an indicator changes. The AI assistant answers questions in natural language, with specialist agents per area, such as sales, finance, HR and marketing, and returns the answer already as a chart.

![AI assistant with specialist agents](/assets/img/decision-suite/assistente-nike.jpg)

![Notification centre with goal alerts](/assets/img/decision-suite/take-06.png)

### I worked with the agents, not just with their screen.

I worked with AI agents on the n8n and Dify platforms, which greatly broadened my view of intelligent automation and assisted experiences, and changed the way I designed the conversation with the assistant. I also collaborated with Qlik partners on creating custom and native dashboards.

### I built the design system together with data engineering.

Working closely with data engineering made me understand the logic and the calls behind each visualisation. That brought into the design system the exceptions a real dashboard has, not just the ideal case. I also investigated edge cases that were not in the initial brief.

#### What changed

### A diffuse problem became a product with scope, order and a language of its own. And it is live.

Each part of the platform answers one of the statements from the interviews. Scattered information gained a single place. The missing team became a guided request. And the dashboard nobody opened became an alert that arrives at the right time.

Today Decision Suite is sold to CEOs and business groups with the same problems we heard in discovery: unreliable data, duplicated sources, generic dashboards, expensive data science and no internal team. The assistant, the agents per area, the goal alerts and the request for new dashboards remain the core of the product.

![Video validation session](/assets/img/decision-suite/take-01.png)

---

#### In hindsight

### Bringing the stakeholders close was what made it possible to translate dense subjects into experiences with a short learning curve. Without them, I would have designed beautiful dashboards for the wrong questions.

### Having the freedom to explore patterns and edge cases was great, but it is also where a product from scratch gets lost. The MoSCoW pipeline was what stopped me from designing everything at once.
